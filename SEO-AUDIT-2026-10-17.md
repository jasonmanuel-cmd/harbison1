## Executive summary

The two-page site has a healthy technical foundation. Both public pages return
200, allow indexing, use self-referencing canonicals, and are included in a valid
sitemap. No crawl-blocking defect was found in the tested routes. The previous
live mobile Lighthouse run scored 100 in all four categories.

The most important next step is to handle the retired pages as a content
retirement rather than redirect every topic to the homepage. Next, verify the
business identity in structured data, finish the homepage snippet cleanly, and
make the bio’s experience claims easier to substantiate.

A Lighthouse SEO score of 100 is a technical checklist result, not evidence that
Google has indexed the site or that it will rank for local real estate searches.

## Technical SEO findings

### 1. Retired content is redirected to a different search intent

**Priority: P1 · Potential impact: High · Confidence: High for behavior; search impact unmeasured**

**Evidence:** `/properties` and `/private-sale` return 301 to `/`;
`/property/2300-weybridge-dr` and `/areas/tehachapi` return 308 to `/`.
These are permanent redirects, but the brand homepage does not replace a
specific property, neighborhood guide, or private-sale explanation.

**Why it matters:** Google warns that redirecting many old URLs to an irrelevant
single destination can be treated as a soft 404. This is a risk, not a confirmed
Search Console error on this site.

**Fix:** Inventory old URLs using Search Console and existing source records.
Keep a permanent redirect where content has an equivalent replacement. For
content deliberately removed without a replacement, return a genuine 404 or
410 with useful navigation. A retired contact page can reasonably point to a
contact section. Do not restore all retired pages simply for SEO, and do not
redirect unrelated content to Call Harbison unless it is a real replacement.

Source: [Google site-move guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes).

### 2. Business schema includes details that need confirmation

**Priority: P2 · Potential impact: Medium · Confidence: High for markup; business-location accuracy unverified**

**Evidence:** Browser-rendered JSON-LD on both pages contains connected
`RealEstateAgent` and `Person` entities; the homepage also has `WebSite` markup.
The business address contains Tehachapi, CA, US without a street or postal code.
The `geo` point is 35.068, -118.4468. `areaServed` includes Newbury Park, which
is not described as a service area in the current page copy. `sameAs` includes
four social accounts but omits the supplied Google Business profile.

The public Google panel matches the business name, phone `(661) 472-7499`,
website, and Kern County description. No street address was displayed in the
panel inspected. A partial address alone is not proof that the JSON-LD is invalid.

**Fix:** Confirm the public service area and business model against Google
Business. Keep only accurate locations and coordinates; avoid representing a
town-center point as a verified office. Add the known Google profile to the
entity’s identity references. If there is a genuine public office, use its
confirmed address. If this is a service-area business, do not invent or expose
a private address merely to complete schema fields. Validate the chosen markup
with Google’s Rich Results Test before publishing it.

Sources: [Google LocalBusiness guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business),
[Organization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization).

### 3. Indexing and migration outcomes need Search Console confirmation

**Priority: P1 for measurement · Potential impact: Unknown · Confidence: Not assessed**

**Evidence:** Public search sampling did not surface the domain in the audit’s
`site:` query results. That is not proof of non-indexation. No Search Console
connection was available, and Google says `site:` results are not exhaustive.

**Fix:** Confirm domain ownership in Search Console, submit the current sitemap,
and inspect both canonical URLs. Review Page Indexing for soft 404s and excluded
URLs, and compare pre-change pages/queries with the current two-page site.
Review manual actions only as a diagnostic check; there is no evidence here of
a penalty. A same-domain redesign does not require a Change of Address request.

Source: [Google’s limits of the site operator](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site).

## On-page findings

### 4. The homepage meta description ends mid-thought

**Priority: P3 · Potential impact: Low · Confidence: High**

**Evidence:** The rendered description ends with `watch Nathanael’s most…`.
The layout clamps the source description at a word boundary, leaving an
incomplete sentence. The bio description is complete and distinct.

**Fix:** Write a complete homepage description that fits without the clamp. For
example: “Harbison Standard brings together real estate, development, and
investing in Kern County. Meet Nathanael Harbison and explore his approach.”

The homepage title is descriptive and not defective, but it could express the
local focus more clearly: “Harbison Standard | Kern County Real Estate”. The bio
can retain its current title or add a natural local descriptor. Character counts
are editing aids, not fixed Google limits; display width determines truncation.

Sources: [Title-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link),
[Snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).

## Content and trust findings

### 5. The biography makes experience claims without direct supporting references

**Priority: P2 · Potential impact: Medium · Confidence: High for the content gap**

**Evidence:** The bio says “verified transaction experience” and mentions probate,
short sales, and divorce-related transactions. The page shows a DRE number and
personal photograph, but does not link to an official license record or offer a
specific, attributable example that substantiates those claims.

**Fix:** Link to the appropriate public DRE lookup for the supplied license number,
after verifying the record. Replace vague “verified” wording with supported facts.
Use one or two approved examples of real work, or connect relevant existing
videos to the background description. Do not invent years in business, credentials,
sales totals, testimonials, or project roles. The audit did not independently
verify license status or every biography claim.

### 6. The Google link description implies reviews that are not currently shown

**Priority: P3 · Potential impact: Low · Confidence: High for the observed panel state**

**Evidence:** The site’s Google Business card says “read client reviews”. The
Google panel opened through the supplied share link displayed “Be the first to
review” during the audit.

**Fix:** Use neutral wording such as “View the Google Business profile” until
reviews are present. Genuine client reviews are a useful longer-term credibility
opportunity. Do not add a rating or review schema from nonexistent or unverified
reviews.

Evidence: [Public Google Business profile](https://share.google/lv8AvSPXSSrPLItKX),
viewed October 17, 2026. Panel presentation can change.

### 7. Visitors have no privacy-information destination

**Priority: P3 · Potential impact: Low for SEO; useful for transparency · Confidence: High**

**Evidence:** `/privacy` permanently redirects to the homepage and no privacy
information is linked in the current footer. The site retains first-party visitor
analytics.

**Fix:** Consider a concise, accurate privacy explanation accessible from the
footer, either in-page or as a small utility page if desired. This is a trust and
transparency recommendation, not a claim that a privacy page is a direct ranking
factor or an assessment of legal compliance.
